# Evidence note v3.74 — quantum geometry as a transport-control axis

## Established external evidence

### Thompson et al. 2025

Primary source: Thompson, J. J. P. et al., *Topologically enhanced exciton transport*, Nature Communications 16, 11448 (2025), DOI `10.1038/s41467-025-66276-9`, CC BY 4.0.

The paper reports theoretical results that:

- topological excitons can have enhanced free, phonon-limited, and polaronic transport;
- quantum geometry contributes to exciton group velocity/diffusion;
- organic polyacene examples show transport enhancement up to approximately four-fold;
- non-uniform electric-field gradients can, in the proposed framework, probe derivatives of the exciton quantum metric;
- dielectric environment, chemistry, and temperature can tune relevant excitonic geometry/topology.

The same paper also makes clear that exciton-phonon scattering is part of the transport problem and that important calculations assume sufficiently large/clean bulk systems. Topology is therefore not treated here as generic disorder immunity.

### Jankowski et al. 2025

Primary source: Jankowski, W. J. et al., *Excitonic topology and quantum geometry in organic semiconductors*, Nature Communications 16, 4661 (2025), DOI `10.1038/s41467-025-59257-5`.

This work establishes the excitonic-topology/quantum-geometry framework used by the later transport paper, including a topology-related lower bound on exciton centre-of-mass spread in the cited one-dimensional setting.

### Yu et al. 2026 — complementary active-control precedent

Primary source: Yu, Y. et al., *Exciton Steering via Potential Landscape Engineered by Excited Electron-Hole Phase Transition*, Physical Review Letters 136, 246901 (2026), DOI `10.1103/sslb-bpc9`.

This different MoS2 platform experimentally demonstrates all-optical exciton steering through an engineered potential landscape. It supports the plausibility of future reconfigurable exciton routing, not the existence of topological excitons in the current project.

## Project inference

The project inference is that **quantum geometry could become a fifth design variable alongside Hamiltonian/coupling, bath/dephasing, cavity/field, and sink**.

The v3.74 first-passage model is not taken from the cited papers. It is an independent project calculation translating any verified diffusion gain into a finite-lifetime sink-capture consequence.

## Synthetic/model result

For 1D diffusion with an absorbing sink and exponential lifetime,

`P_capture = exp[-L/sqrt(D tau)]`.

At dimensionless sink distance `x=2`, a synthetic 4× diffusion gain increases capture from `0.1353` to `0.3679`, a factor `2.7183`.

This is dimensionless model arithmetic. No project material is assigned `x=2` or `G=4`.

## Strong conventional alternatives

A future apparent 'topological' advantage could instead arise from:

- ordinary band dispersion;
- different exciton lifetime;
- different crystal quality or defect density;
- morphology/packing;
- chemical substitution changing coupling or reorganization energy;
- dielectric screening;
- free-carrier drift or exciton dissociation in applied fields.

A valid experiment must directly distinguish these before a quantum-geometric mechanism claim.
