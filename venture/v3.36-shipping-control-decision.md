# Venture decision — v3.36 shipping-control gate

## Decision

Do not treat a future A→B→A R2 transfer result as procurement-, partnership-, or multi-site-pooling ready until shipment/handling state change is either prospectively controlled or explicitly bounded.

## Why this matters commercially

A false facility-comparison conclusion is expensive in both directions:

- falsely blaming a partner lab can trigger unnecessary requalification, duplicate capex, or lost collaboration;
- falsely accepting agreement can hide a transport/packaging weakness that later appears as unexplained measurement drift.

The v3.36 TRAVEL/HOME design is intended as a low-complexity diagnostic before committing to a larger cross-facility campaign.

## Planning burden

The nominal synthetic planning case requires six independent substrates in each arm to exceed 80% normal-approximation power for a 5 mV shipping effect when substrate-level PRE-to-POST change SD is assumed to be 3 mV.

This is **not a vendor quote, measured yield, or universal sample-size requirement**. Sensitivity is large: the same calculation requires 3/6/11/16 substrates per arm when the assumed change SD is 2/3/4/5 mV. Real variance must replace the synthetic assumption as soon as a pilot exists.

A three-per-arm screen has only ~53.24% synthetic power under the nominal model. Therefore a tiny favorable pilot should not be marketed as evidence that the transfer process is robust.

## Spend gate

Proceed to a full paid multi-site transfer only if:

1. the v3.35 carrier/insert passes dummy physical qualification;
2. the v3.36 raw/provenance packet is complete;
3. HOME and TRAVEL arms use the same frozen facility-A measurement configuration;
4. adverse travel changes are retained rather than excluded post hoc;
5. the result is reviewed jointly with carrier/package/deviation records;
6. any observed shipping effect small enough to permit cross-facility interpretation is described narrowly as configuration-specific screening evidence, not universal transport robustness.

## Kill/narrow

If travel produces a material or highly uncertain adverse shift relative to HOME controls, pause cross-facility pooling and diagnose packaging, handling, environment, contacts, encapsulation, remounting, and elapsed-time imbalance. Preserve the negative result; do not solve it by selecting only stable travelers.

## Single best next increment

Run one randomized HOME/TRAVEL dry transfer at the home facility using the qualified carrier and record actual substrate-level PRE/POST variance. That empirical variance is the highest-value input for resizing the later facility-transfer cohort and replacing the synthetic 3 mV assumption.
