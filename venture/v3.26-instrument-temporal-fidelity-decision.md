# v3.26 deployment decision — qualify the instrument before interpreting settling

## Decision

Do not treat a slow R2 intensity-step response as source/DUT physics until the configured electrical acquisition path has passed an independent temporal-fidelity check.

## Why this matters

The program is now operating at tens-of-microvolts residual-settling tolerances. Fixed filtering, aperture averaging, timestamp/trigger error, autorange, compliance transitions, and firmware buffering can be comparable to or larger than that budget. Spending on more DUT repeats before bounding the acquisition path would not improve inference.

## Current project allocation

Pending review of open v3.25, reserve 20% of its 69.5369 uV pointwise settling envelope for acquisition-chain temporal error: 13.9074 uV. This is a provisional engineering allocation, not a standards-derived tolerance.

## Practical handoff

A facility should provide at minimum:
- exact instrument model/firmware/software versions;
- fixed range, filter, aperture/integration time, sample cadence, and trigger configuration;
- autorange disabled;
- characterized reference electrical step and uncertainty;
- timestamp uncertainty or traceable timing characterization;
- at least six repeated step records spanning beyond intended R2 dwell.

## Kill / narrow rule

If the configured instrument chain cannot demonstrably enter and remain within its allocated error envelope before the intended DUT dwell, lengthen dwell/change acquisition configuration and requalify. Do not deconvolve aggressively or subtract an assumed transient merely to rescue a mechanism claim.

## Cost implication

This is a low-capital discriminator compared with additional device fabrication or spectroscopy. It should be completed before attributing slow dynamics to device physics and before facility time is spent on large randomized/settling campaigns.

## Claim boundary

Instrument qualification is metrology infrastructure. It does not establish energy gain, EPC control, recombination physics, or open-quantum transport.
