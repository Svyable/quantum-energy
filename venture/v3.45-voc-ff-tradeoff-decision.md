# v3.45 venture decision — optimize the voltage-loss/FF frontier, not EPC alone

## Decision

Treat **field robustness of charge generation** as an explicit Stage-1/Stage-2 bridge variable.

A lower EPC/reorganization metric or lower non-radiative voltage loss is valuable only if the same process state preserves charge generation and does not introduce a fill-factor penalty large enough to erase useful work.

## Why this changes capital allocation

The 2026 Nature Photonics evidence shows that low-voltage-loss OSCs can encounter a field-dependent-generation FF limit. The v3.45 synthetic Marcus–Stark audit independently demonstrates that reducing reorganization energy at a fixed driving force can increase local rate sensitivity rather than monotonically improve it.

Therefore fabrication/process optimization should target a **bounded energetic/EPC window** and the final electrical frontier, not a single microscopic minimum.

## Updated pre-spend logic

Before scaling a promising B1/B2 recipe:

1. confirm the intended interface/EPC/reorganization movement;
2. measure DeltaVnr/Voc under qualified metrology;
3. measure field-dependent generation with bias-dependent PL and preferably TDCF or a justified equivalent;
4. measure stabilized FF and Pmax;
5. retain morphology, contact, optical and transport nulls;
6. require same-direction useful-work improvement across independent lots before scale/cavity spend.

## Kill/narrow rule

If a recipe reduces voltage loss but increases field-dependent generation loss and does not improve stabilized FF/Pmax, classify it as mechanism science rather than a deployable process-control win.

## Business implication

The defensible platform target becomes more valuable, not less narrow: a controller should learn a **multi-objective process window** that balances interface state, voltage loss, field robustness, FF, Pmax and durability. A controller that merely minimizes an EPC proxy risks optimizing the wrong objective.

No revenue, efficiency, lifetime or valuation claim changes in this increment.
