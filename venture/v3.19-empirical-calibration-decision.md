# v3.19 open deployment decision — estimate covariance before spending on mechanism

## Decision

Before treating a facility's R2 `Voc`-intensity curvature as confirmatory, require repeated reference-detector calibration sweeps sufficient to estimate the facility's calibration repeatability covariance with v3.19.

The preferred sequence is now:

1. v3.17 — acquisition-path qualification;
2. v3.19 — empirical calibration-component estimation from repeated reference sweeps;
3. v3.18 — covariance propagation into `Delta n_curv` and the frozen v3.15 power decision;
4. only then — mechanism interpretation together with FTPS/EL/temperature/contact controls.

## Why it matters

This prevents two opposite errors:

- over-penalizing harmless common-mode intensity scale as if every point were independent;
- underestimating smooth correlated shape/drift modes that can actually bias a derivative observable.

## Capital implication

No new instrument purchase is justified yet. The next spend should buy **repeat calibration data and provenance**, not more modeling complexity or additional R2 substrates before the measurement covariance is empirically known.

If the empirical calibration contribution remains small relative to `Voc` uncertainty, effort should shift to improving `Voc` precision and substrate replication. If smooth calibration modes dominate, calibration/source control becomes the higher-value intervention.

## Open-source value

A laboratory can publish its repeated reference sweeps plus the v3.19 JSON/sidecar, allowing another group to rerun the same decomposition and propagate it with v3.18. The absolute detector calibration certificate remains a separate required artifact because repeatability cannot determine absolute systematic bias.

## Claim boundary

This is metrology/process infrastructure. It is not evidence of quantum transport, EPC control, energy gain, or a unique recombination mechanism.
